Recent History Exam

New at the Canadian Championships for 2027 · Varsity / Junior Varsity

What this exam covers

Historical developments since January 1, 2000 — political, social, cultural, economic and scientific history of the last quarter-century:

  • Wars and geopolitics: Iraq, Afghanistan, Syria, Ukraine, Israel-Gaza
  • Leaders, elections and movements worldwide
  • Technology and science milestones: smartphones, social media, AI, spaceflight, COVID-19
  • Economics: 2008 financial crisis, eurozone, crypto
  • Culture and sport: Olympics, World Cups, major deaths and prizes

About these practice papers

Recent History is new at the Canadian Championships for 2027, so no Canadian past papers exist. The sets below are the same exam from the Asian Championships (2026 and 2024) and the US National Championships (2023 and 2022) — identical format, scoring and scope, and the closest available exposure to how this exam is actually written.

How to prepare beyond papers

Build a mental timeline 2000 → today, year by year: one war/conflict, one election or leader change, one disaster, one technology launch, one sporting event per year is a strong skeleton. Current-affairs reading compounds quickly in this exam.

Exam strategy (read once, use every paper)

  • Scoring: +2 correct, -1 wrong, 0 blank. A blind guess among 4 options loses points on average (-0.25), so leave it blank unless you can eliminate at least one option. Narrowed to two options, guessing is profitable (+0.5 average); narrowed to three, it is break-even.
  • Pacing: 75 questions in 25 minutes is 20 seconds per question. First pass: answer everything you know cold and flag the rest. Second pass: work the flagged ones with elimination. Never let one question eat a minute.
  • Tiebreaker: the real exam ends with one numerical tiebreaker (unpenalized, must be attempted). It is not included in these practice sets.
